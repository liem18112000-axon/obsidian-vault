# Cost per Lead

> [!summary]
> **Cost per Lead (CPL)** is an affiliate pricing model where the advertiser pays the affiliate a fixed amount each time referred traffic becomes a **lead** — a prospect who hands over contact details or expresses interest — *without* requiring a purchase. It is a sub-type of [[Cost per Action]] where the "action" is lead generation, not a sale.

## Plain-English definition

You send traffic to a merchant. Under CPL, you get paid the moment a visitor **signs up, submits a form, requests a quote, books a demo, or joins a list** — i.e. becomes a *lead*. Crucially, **the visitor does not have to buy anything.** Converting that lead into a paying customer is the merchant's job, not yours.

This is the key difference from [[Cost per Sale]]: CPL pays on *interest*, CPS pays on *purchase*.

## Where CPL sits

CPL is one of the three common [[Cost per Action]] flavours:

| Action that triggers payout | Model | Payout shape | Typical payout size |
|-----------------------------|-------|--------------|---------------------|
| Becomes a lead (sign-up, form) | **CPL** | Flat per lead | Low–medium |
| Makes a purchase | [[Cost per Sale]] (CPS) | Flat or % of sale | Medium–high |
| Any defined event (umbrella) | [[Cost per Action]] (CPA) | Flat or % | Varies |

Because the lead hasn't paid the merchant yet, **per-lead payouts are lower** than per-sale payouts — the merchant is buying a *maybe*, not a confirmed customer.

## How a CPL payout works

1. Affiliate places a tracked link.
2. Visitor clicks; a [[Cookie Duration|cookie]] records the affiliate's ID.
3. Visitor completes the **lead action** — e.g. fills a "request a quote" form, starts a free trial, subscribes to a newsletter.
4. The lead is validated (is it real? duplicate? junk?).
5. Once approved, the flat **CPL payout** is credited — e.g. *$8 per validated email sign-up*, *$40 per insurance quote request*.

## Worked example

You run a personal-finance blog. A lender offers **$25 CPL** for each completed loan-eligibility form, with a 30-day cookie.

- 5,000 readers, 400 click your link (8%).
- 60 complete the eligibility form.
- Payout: `60 × $25 = $1,500` — **even if none of them ever take a loan.**

The merchant accepts this because, on average, enough of those 60 leads will convert downstream to make $25 each worthwhile.

## What counts as a "lead" (define it precisely)

CPL offers live or die on the **lead definition**. Watch for:

- **Single opt-in vs double opt-in** — does an email count immediately, or only after the visitor confirms?
- **Required fields** — name + email is a cheap lead; full application with income + phone is an expensive one.
- **Qualification gates** — some offers only pay for leads in certain countries, age ranges, or credit tiers.

> [!tip] Lead quality is the whole game
> A merchant tracks how many of your leads actually convert. Send junk and they'll lower your rate, cap you, or drop you. The best CPL affiliates pre-qualify visitors *before* the form, so the leads they send are warm.

## Common gotchas

> [!warning] CPL is the most fraud-sensitive model
> Because a "lead" is cheap to fake (bots filling forms, incentivised junk sign-ups), CPL offers have **strict validation, scrubbing, and reversal** rules. Expect a hold period and expect some leads to be rejected. Your *paid* leads will be fewer than your *submitted* leads.

- **Scrubbing / reversal** — invalid, duplicate, or low-quality leads are removed and not paid (see [[Reversal]] / clawback).
- **Caps** — many CPL offers limit daily/monthly leads to control budget.
- **Attribution** — usually last-click; see [[Attribution Model]].

## When CPL is the right model to chase

- The merchant has a **longer sales cycle** (insurance, mortgages, B2B SaaS, education) where the purchase happens offline or weeks later — they'd rather pay for the lead now than track the eventual sale.
- Your audience is willing to **fill a form** but not necessarily buy on the spot.
- You can **pre-qualify** traffic so your lead quality stays high.

If the product is a simple one-click purchase, [[Cost per Sale]] usually pays you more per conversion. CPL shines where the *commitment to buy* is too far down the funnel to track reliably.

## Related terms

- [[Cost per Action]] — the parent model; CPL is the lead-generation flavour.
- [[Cost per Sale]] — the sibling model that pays on purchase, not interest.
- [[Earnings per Click]] — the metric to compare CPL offers (folds payout × conversion).
- [[Cookie Duration]] — the window in which your referred lead still counts.
- [[Attribution Model]] — decides which affiliate gets credited for the lead.
- [[Reversal]] — clawback of leads that fail validation.
