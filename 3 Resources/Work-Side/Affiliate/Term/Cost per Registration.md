# Cost per Registration

> [!summary]
> **Cost per Registration (CPR)** is an affiliate pricing model where the advertiser pays a fixed amount each time a referred visitor **creates an account / registers** — no purchase required. It is a sub-type of [[Cost per Action]] where the triggering action is a completed registration (sign-up).

## Plain-English definition

You send traffic to a merchant. Under CPR you're paid the moment a visitor **completes a registration** — opens an account, signs up for a free tier, creates a profile, registers for a webinar or platform. They don't have to pay or even deposit anything. The bar is simply: *a new registered user exists because of you.*

## CPR vs the lookalike models

CPR is easy to confuse with [[Cost per Lead]] — the distinction is **what the user committed to**:

| Model | Triggering action | Commitment level |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| [[Cost per Lead]] (CPL) | Submits contact info / interest (form, quote) | Low — gave details |
| **Cost per Registration (CPR)** | **Creates a working account** | **Medium — set up a usable login** |
| [[Cost per Sale]] (CPS) | Makes a purchase | High — paid money |

A **lead** hands over details; a **registration** actually provisions an account the user can log into. CPR sits between CPL and CPS: more commitment than a form fill, less than a payment.

> [!note] CPR is a CPA flavour, not a separate family
> Like [[Cost per Lead]] and [[Cost per Sale]], CPR is just [[Cost per Action]] with the "action" pinned to *account creation*.

## How a CPR payout works

1. Affiliate places a tracked link.
2. Visitor clicks; a [[Cookie Duration|cookie]] records the affiliate's ID.
3. Visitor completes the **registration** — verifies email, sets a password, account is live.
4. Registration is validated (real person? verified email? not a duplicate?).
5. Once approved, the flat **CPR payout** is credited — e.g. *$3 per verified signup*, *$15 per completed broker account*.

## Where CPR is common

- **Freemium SaaS** — pay per free-tier signup, hoping to upsell later.
- **iGaming / trading / fintech** — pay per registered account (often a step before a separate "first deposit" payout under a hybrid deal).
- **Apps** — "cost per install + registration" where the install alone isn't enough; the user must create an account.
- **Marketplaces / communities** — pay to grow the registered user base.

## Worked example

A trading platform offers **$12 CPR** for each verified account registration, 30-day cookie.

- You drive 600 clicks, 90 start registration, 50 verify and complete it.
- Payout: `50 × $12 = $600` — regardless of whether any of them ever deposit or trade.

The platform accepts this because a share of registered users will fund accounts later; CPR buys the top of *their* funnel.

## Common gotchas

> [!warning] A registration is cheap to fake — expect heavy validation
> Like [[Cost per Lead]], CPR is fraud-prone (bot signups, throwaway emails, incentivised junk accounts). Offers defend with email/phone verification, de-duplication, and [[Reversal|scrubbing]]. Your *paid* registrations will be fewer than your *submitted* ones.

- **"Registration" must be defined.** Does an unverified email count, or only a confirmed, logged-in account? Pin this down.
- **Often the lower half of a hybrid deal.** Many iGaming/fintech programs pay a small CPR *plus* a larger [[Cost per Action|CPA]]/[[Revenue Share]] on first deposit — don't mistake the registration crumb for the whole payout.
- **Quality affects your rate.** Merchants track how many registrations activate; low activation invites rate cuts or caps (compare [[Cost per Quality Lead]] logic).
- **Attribution** — typically last-click; see [[Attribution Model]].

## When CPR is the right model to chase

- The merchant runs a **freemium or deposit-later funnel** and values raw registered users.
- Your audience will **sign up for free** but isn't ready to pay — registration is a realistic ask.
- You want **higher volume / lower friction** payouts than [[Cost per Sale]], accepting a smaller per-action amount.

If the merchant only profits on payment (and tracks it well), [[Cost per Sale]] or a deposit-based hybrid usually pays you more per converted user than a bare registration.

## Related terms

- [[Cost per Action]] — the parent model; CPR is the account-creation flavour.
- [[Cost per Lead]] — lower-commitment sibling (details, not an account).
- [[Cost per Sale]] — higher-commitment sibling (payment).
- [[Cost per Quality Lead]] — same quality-gating logic applies to "active" registrations.
- [[Revenue Share]] — often paired with CPR in hybrid iGaming/fintech deals.
- [[Earnings per Click]] — the metric to compare CPR offers (use the *verified* registration rate).
- [[Cookie Duration]] — the window in which a referred registration still counts.
- [[Attribution Model]] — decides which affiliate gets credited.
- [[Reversal]] — invalid registrations are scrubbed and clawed back.
