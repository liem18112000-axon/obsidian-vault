# Revenue Share

> [!summary]
> **Revenue Share (RevShare)** is an affiliate pricing model where the affiliate earns a **recurring percentage of the ongoing revenue** a referred customer generates — not a one-off payout. As long as the customer keeps paying the merchant, the affiliate keeps earning. It is the recurring counterpart to the one-time [[Cost per Sale]].

## Plain-English definition

Under [[Cost per Sale]] you're paid **once** when the customer buys. Under RevShare you're paid **every billing cycle** for as long as that customer stays. Refer a subscriber to a $30/month SaaS at 20% RevShare and you earn $6/month — month after month — until they cancel.

It turns a single referral into an **income stream** instead of a one-time event.

## Where RevShare fits

| Model | Payout timing | Best for |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| [[Cost per Lead]] | Once, on lead | Lead-gen, long sales cycles |
| [[Cost per Sale]] | Once, on purchase | Retail, one-off products |
| **Revenue Share** | **Recurring, every cycle** | **Subscriptions, SaaS, memberships, hosting, casinos/finance** |

RevShare only makes sense where the customer **pays repeatedly** — subscriptions, SaaS, web hosting, membership sites, trading/iGaming platforms. For one-off physical products there's nothing recurring to share.

## How RevShare works

1. Affiliate refers a customer who subscribes.
2. Each billing cycle the customer pays the merchant.
3. The affiliate earns the agreed **% of that cycle's revenue** (e.g. 20–40%).
4. Earnings continue until the customer **churns** (cancels) — or, in some programs, until a fixed term ends.

## The maths: LTV beats one-off payout (eventually)

The key comparison is **recurring RevShare vs a one-time bounty**:

> [!tip] RevShare wins on retention, loses on churn
> - **One-off [[Cost per Sale]]:** $50 flat per signup — paid once, certain.
> - **RevShare:** 25% of $30/month = $7.50/month.
>   - Customer stays 3 months → $22.50 (RevShare loses)
>   - Customer stays 12 months → $90 (RevShare wins big)
>   - Customer stays 3 years → $270 (RevShare crushes it)
>
> The break-even here is ~7 months. RevShare pays off only if customers **retain**. Low-churn products (essential SaaS, hosting) favour RevShare; high-churn ones favour taking the flat bounty.

This is why RevShare value tracks the customer's **[[Lifetime Value|lifetime value (LTV)]]**: your earnings ≈ `RevShare % × monthly price × average customer lifetime`.

## Variants

- **Lifetime RevShare** — you earn for the entire life of the customer. Best case.
- **Fixed-term RevShare** — recurring, but capped (e.g. first 12 months only).
- **Hybrid** — a one-time bounty *plus* a smaller recurring share (common in SaaS: e.g. $100 upfront + 10% recurring).
- **Tiered RevShare** — the % rises as you refer more active customers.

## Common gotchas

> [!warning] Churn and clawbacks erode the stream
> - **Early cancellation** can trigger a [[Reversal]] of the first cycle(s) if the customer churns inside a hold period.
> - **Customer leaves → income stops.** RevShare income is only as durable as the merchant's retention; promote sticky products.
> - **Merchant risk.** If the merchant raises prices, degrades the product, or shuts down, your passive stream shrinks or vanishes — you're exposed to *their* business health, not just your traffic.
> - **Attribution longevity.** Confirm the program credits *you* for the customer's lifetime, not just the first payment — read whether it's true lifetime or fixed-term.

## When RevShare is the right model to chase

- Promoting **subscription / SaaS / membership** products with **low churn**.
- You want to **compound** earnings — build a stack of recurring customers rather than restart the hunt every month.
- The product is **sticky** (hard to switch away from), so customers retain for years.

If the product churns fast, or it's a one-time purchase, a flat [[Cost per Sale]] bounty is the safer, often larger, choice.

## Related terms

- [[Cost per Sale]] — the one-time counterpart; RevShare trades certainty now for more later.
- [[Cost per Action]] — the umbrella of one-off action-based payouts RevShare contrasts with.
- [[Reversal]] — early churn can claw back recent RevShare cycles.
- [[Earnings per Click]] — for RevShare, EPC must be modelled over customer lifetime, not a single payout.
- [[Attribution Model]] — determines whether you keep earning on the customer long-term.
