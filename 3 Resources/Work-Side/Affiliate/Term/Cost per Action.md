# Cost per Action

> [!summary]
> **Cost per Action (CPA)** is an affiliate pricing model where the advertiser pays the affiliate only when a referred visitor completes a specific, predefined **action** — not merely for clicks or impressions. The action is the thing the advertiser actually values: a sale, a sign-up, a form submission, an app install.

## Plain-English definition

You (the affiliate) send traffic to a merchant. Under CPA, you get paid **only if that traffic does something concrete** — buys a product, registers an account, books a demo, downloads an app. No action, no payout, no matter how many people clicked.

It is the most **performance-aligned** model in affiliate marketing: the advertiser pays for results, and carries almost none of the upfront risk.

> [!note] CPA = Cost per Action = Cost per Acquisition
> The same acronym is used both ways. "Acquisition" usually implies the action is acquiring a *customer/lead*; "Action" is the broader umbrella that also covers non-purchase events. In affiliate contexts they're used interchangeably.

## Where it sits among the pricing models

| Model | Advertiser pays when… | Who carries the risk |
|-------|-----------------------|----------------------|
| CPM (cost per mille) | Ad is shown 1,000 times | Advertiser |
| CPC (cost per click) | Visitor clicks the link | Shared |
| **CPA (cost per action)** | **Visitor completes a defined action** | **Affiliate** |
| CPL (cost per lead) | Visitor becomes a lead (a *type* of CPA) | Affiliate |
| CPS (cost per sale) | Visitor makes a purchase (a *type* of CPA) | Affiliate |
| RevShare | Recurring % of revenue the customer generates | Affiliate |

CPL and CPS are really **sub-types of CPA** — they just name which action triggers the payout. The further down this table, the more the risk shifts from advertiser to affiliate, and (usually) the higher the per-action payout to compensate.

## How a CPA payout works, step by step

1. Affiliate places a tracked link (with their unique ID) on their site, video, email, or ad.
2. A visitor clicks it. A **cookie** is dropped on the visitor's device recording the affiliate's ID and a timestamp.
3. The visitor lands on the merchant's site.
4. If, within the **cookie window** (e.g. 30 days), the visitor completes the defined action (say, a purchase), the network attributes that action to the affiliate.
5. The action enters a **pending / validation** period (to allow for returns, fraud checks, cancellations).
6. Once approved, the **CPA payout** — a flat amount or a % of sale value — is credited to the affiliate.

> [!tip] The two CPA flavours
> - **Flat CPA** — fixed cash per action, e.g. *$45 per completed sign-up*. Common for leads, app installs, SaaS trials.
> - **Percentage CPA (a.k.a. CPS)** — a cut of the sale value, e.g. *10% of order total*. Common for physical-product retail.

## A worked example

You run a review site. A merchant offers **$50 CPA** for each paid subscription, with a **30-day cookie**.

- 10,000 people read your review.
- 800 click your affiliate link (8% click-through).
- 40 subscribe within 30 days (5% conversion of clicks).
- Payout: `40 × $50 = $2,000`.

Notice: the 9,200 who didn't click and the 760 who clicked but didn't subscribe cost the advertiser **nothing**. That's the defining feature of CPA.

## Why advertisers love it (and affiliates should respect it)

- **Advertiser upside:** near-zero wasted spend. Every dollar maps to a tangible result, so ROI is easy to prove.
- **Affiliate trade-off:** you take on the conversion risk. You can drive 100,000 clicks and earn $0 if none convert. This rewards affiliates who send *qualified, high-intent* traffic — which is exactly why [[Cost per Action]] pairs so well with bottom-of-funnel buyer content (best-X listicles, reviews, comparisons).

## Key terms that govern your real CPA earnings

The headline CPA rate is only half the story. These modifiers decide what you actually keep:

- **Cookie duration / window** — how long after the click the action still counts to you. Longer = more credited actions.
- **Attribution model** — usually *last-click* (the last affiliate before the action wins). Determines who gets paid when multiple affiliates touched the visitor.
- **Validation / hold period** — delay before the action is confirmed (covers refunds and fraud).
- **Reversal / clawback** — if the customer refunds or cancels, the action is voided and the payout is taken back.
- **EPC (earnings per click)** — `total CPA earnings ÷ total clicks`. The single best metric for comparing CPA offers, because it folds the payout rate *and* the conversion rate into one number.

> [!warning] CPA rate alone is a trap
> A $100 CPA offer that converts at 0.5% earns less per click than a $20 CPA offer that converts at 5%. Always compare offers on **EPC**, not headline payout.

## When CPA is the right model to chase

- You produce **high-intent content** (reviews, "best of", comparisons) where readers are close to buying.
- The merchant's product has a **clear, trackable conversion event**.
- You can tolerate **delayed, validated payouts** rather than getting paid per click.

If your traffic is top-of-funnel and curiosity-driven, CPC or display (CPM) may monetise better than CPA, because those visitors rarely complete actions.
